# Rarely Marketing Plan

## 1. Objective

Validate that pet owners will pay for and share a personalized Rarely card, then
build a repeatable organic acquisition loop before spending meaningfully on ads.

### First 90-day targets

| Outcome | Day 30 | Day 60 | Day 90 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Paid five-card packs | 50 | 150 cumulative | 350 cumulative |
| Completed cards | 150 | 500 cumulative | 1,200 cumulative |
| Card share rate | 20% | 25% | 30% |
| Purchase-to-first-card rate | 70% | 80% | 85% |
| Repeat purchase rate within 30 days | Measure | 8% | 12% |
| Customer support or refund rate | <8% | <6% | <5% |

These are validation targets, not forecasts. Review them weekly and change them
after the first 100 purchases establish a real baseline.

## 2. Product and positioning

**Product:** Rarely turns one cat or dog photo into a downloadable 9:16 rarity
card with a name, title, rarity score, signature trait, ratings, and an imagined
inner voice. Five attempts cost $5 as a one-time purchase; there is no
subscription.

**Primary audience:** English-speaking cat and dog owners aged 18–44 who already
post, save, or send pet photos.

**Best early-adopter segments:**

1. Pet owners whose friends already recognize their pet's distinct personality.
2. Pet-focused creators with 2,000–50,000 followers.
3. Multi-pet households, because a five-card pack has an obvious use.
4. People marking an adoption anniversary, birthday, or pet memorial.

**Positioning statement:**

> For pet owners who know every look their pet makes, Rarely turns one photo
> into a funny, heartfelt one-of-one card that feels unmistakably like them.

**Core promise:** "The card that makes everyone say, 'that is so them.'"

**Reasons to believe:**

- The result reads the pet's expression, posture, and surroundings.
- Every completed result is a polished, downloadable 9:16 card.
- Five attempts cost $5 once, with no subscription.
- Photos and cards remain private unless the owner chooses to share them.
- Purchases have a 14-day full-refund guarantee.

**Positioning guardrail:** Never use Pokémon, Pokédex, or another game or card
brand in public copy, keywords, hashtags, visuals, or comparisons. Use
"one-of-one pet card" and "pet rarity card." Describe the result as
entertainment, not an objective assessment of a pet.

## 3. Message hierarchy

Lead with the emotional result, not the technology.

1. **Recognition:** "You know that look. Rarely puts it into words."
2. **Output:** A one-of-one pet card made to save, send, or post.
3. **Value:** Five card attempts for $5.
4. **Risk reversal:** One payment, no subscription, 14-day refund guarantee.
5. **Trust:** Private upload; the owner decides whether to share.
6. **Mechanism:** AI reads visible details to create the card.

### Reusable hooks

- "We turned the photo everyone knows into the card only they could have."
- "What does your pet think they are famous for?"
- "Send us their most suspicious photo."
- "This dog scored 94 rarity. His reason was not what we expected."
- "Five pets. Five cards. One household ranking."
- "The face says one thing. Their card says the rest."

### Calls to action

- Primary: **Make their card**
- Value-led: **Get 5 card credits — $5**
- Social: **Show us the look only you understand**

Avoid vague calls to action such as "Try AI" or "Generate now."

## 4. Acquisition strategy

### Channel 1: Short-form organic video — 50% of effort

Publish to Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Reuse the same concept,
but upload natively without another platform's watermark.

Recommended formats:

- Photo first, then animated card reveal.
- Owner reads the imagined inner voice and reacts.
- "Guess the rarity" before revealing the score.
- Two pets compared without declaring a winner.
- A weekly theme: side-eye, stolen seat, tiny criminal, dramatic sleeper.
- Screen recording showing photo-to-card in under 15 seconds.

Cadence: four short videos and two image carousels per week for eight weeks.
Every post should show the finished card in the first two seconds or create a
clear reveal within five seconds.

### Channel 2: Pet micro-creators — 25% of effort

Build a list of 100 creators whose audiences comment on the pet's personality,
not merely its appearance. Contact 15–20 per week.

Offer a complimentary five-card pack with no posting requirement. Ask for
permission to repost only after they receive a result they like. For creators
who post successfully, test a manually tracked 20% revenue share before
building a full affiliate system.

Sample outreach:

> I built Rarely because some pet photos already feel like an inside joke.
> I would love to make [pet name] a one-of-one rarity card. I can gift you five
> attempts—no posting obligation. If one feels exactly like them, may we share
> it with credit?

Track creator name, audience size, contact date, UTM link, cards completed,
posts, visits, and purchases in one sheet.

### Channel 3: Customer sharing — 15% of effort

The downloadable 9:16 card is the growth asset. Encourage sharing without
placing a large promotional watermark over the keepsake.

- Ask for a share immediately after a successful result.
- Provide an optional caption and `rarely.cards` attribution.
- Request permission to feature strong cards in a weekly "Rarely Seen" post.
- After the third completed card, prompt users to show their household set.
- Test a referral reward only after attribution and abuse controls exist.

### Channel 4: Communities and partnerships — 10% of effort

- Participate in breed, adoption, and local pet communities as a person first.
- Ask moderators before posting a product or giveaway.
- Offer shelters a set of cards for adoptable animals and provide reusable
  social templates. Do not imply a formal charity partnership without one.
- Pitch pet newsletters and small podcasts with an owner story or themed card
  collection, not a generic launch announcement.

Do not mass-post links or use generated comments. Community trust is worth more
than a temporary traffic spike.

### Google Play and search

Use the Play listing as conversion support rather than the main discovery
channel at launch.

- Title: **Rarely: Pet Rarity Cards**
- Short description: **Turn one cat or dog photo into a funny, one-of-one card.**
- Screenshots should tell one story: upload, personality read, finished card,
  save/share, five credits with no subscription.
- Ask for a rating only after a user successfully completes and saves a card.
- Build search pages only for genuine intent, such as pet birthday cards or
  personalized digital pet keepsakes. Do not create thin breed pages at scale.

## 5. Launch sequence

### Phase 0 — Measurement and assets (week 0)

Before sending meaningful traffic:

- Instrument the funnel events listed below.
- Add UTM capture and retain first-touch and last-touch attribution through
  purchase.
- Prepare at least 20 approved example cards across cats, dogs, lighting
  conditions, and personality types.
- Produce 12 short videos, 6 carousels, and 3 creator outreach examples.
- Create a support response for poor photo fit, refunds, privacy, and credits.
- Add a simple way to gift or manually grant creator credits.
- Verify the complete web and Google Play purchase flows.

### Phase 1 — Private beta (weeks 1–2)

- Recruit 20–30 pet owners from personal networks and small pet communities.
- Give each tester a defined task: make at least two cards and share one if they
  genuinely like it.
- Interview ten testers for the exact words they use to describe the result.
- Fix repeated confusion before increasing traffic.
- Select the five strongest permission-cleared transformations for launch.

Exit criteria:

- At least 70% of purchasers complete a first card.
- At least 20% of completed-card users use Share or download.
- Fewer than 10% of attempts produce a repeated, preventable complaint.
- Payment, credit return, refund, and privacy flows work reliably.

### Phase 2 — Public launch (weeks 3–4)

- Publish one founder video explaining the insight behind Rarely.
- Release three strong pet transformations over the first 48 hours.
- Contact 40 selected micro-creators with personalized messages.
- Post the launch to relevant founder communities, but keep pet-owner content
  focused on the pets rather than the product launch.
- Feature customer cards daily only with explicit permission.
- Reply to comments using new card examples instead of sales copy.

### Phase 3 — Find repeatability (weeks 5–8)

- Group content by hook, pet type, format, and creator.
- Repeat concepts that produce purchases or shares, not merely views.
- Test the top three landing-page messages one at a time.
- Start shelter or adoption partnerships with one small pilot.
- Boost only organic posts that already produce high-intent visits.

### Phase 4 — Controlled scale (weeks 9–12)

- Negotiate paid packages with creators who already drove purchases.
- Add a referral mechanism if customer sharing converts.
- Expand the best two content series into a weekly schedule.
- Localize the Play listing only where actual traffic and support capacity exist.
- Raise spend only when customer acquisition cost remains below the approved
  contribution-margin ceiling.

## 6. Content system

### Weekly cadence

| Day | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Before-and-after card reveal |
| Tuesday | Creator or customer card with permission |
| Wednesday | "Guess their rarity" interactive post |
| Thursday | Product process or founder post |
| Friday | Themed pet-photo prompt |
| Saturday | Best reaction, comment response, or household set |
| Sunday | Review results and prepare next week's variants |

### Content rules

- The pet and card are always the visual focus.
- Show real outputs; do not silently rewrite weak results for an ad.
- Obtain written permission before using customer photos or testimonials.
- Do not manufacture reactions, scarcity, reviews, or rarity statistics.
- Never shame a pet or suggest a low score means lower worth.
- Keep AI disclosure available and clear, without making "AI" the headline.

## 7. Funnel and measurement

### Required events

1. `landing_view` with source, medium, campaign, creative, and platform.
2. `photo_selected`.
3. `auth_started` and `auth_completed`.
4. `checkout_started`.
5. `purchase_completed` with channel and localized revenue.
6. `analysis_started`, `analysis_accepted`, and `analysis_rejected`.
7. `card_completed`.
8. `card_shared` and `card_downloaded`.
9. `second_card_completed`.
10. `repeat_purchase_completed`.
11. `refund_requested` and `refund_completed`.

Do not send pet names, email addresses, photos, generated text, or other
personal content to analytics.

### Weekly dashboard

- Visitors by source and campaign.
- Visitor-to-photo, photo-to-checkout, and checkout-to-purchase conversion.
- Purchase-to-first-card completion.
- Accepted versus rejected analysis rate.
- Share/download rate per completed user.
- Repeat purchase rate.
- Gross revenue, refunds, payment fees, AI cost, and contribution margin.
- Customer acquisition cost by channel.
- Top five creatives by purchases and contribution, not clicks.

### Decision rules

- Do not scale an ad or creator based on views alone.
- Pause a paid source after enough traffic to judge if acquisition cost exceeds
  first-purchase contribution and there is no evidence of repeat purchases.
- Investigate any funnel step that loses more than half of users.
- If sharing is below 20%, interview users before building referrals.
- If purchase conversion is weak but card interest is high, test trust and
  value messaging before discounting.

## 8. Budget

Start with a maximum test budget of **$300–$500 for the first month**:

| Use | Budget |
| --- | ---: |
| Creator seeding and small proven-creator fees | $150–$250 |
| Boosting already successful organic posts | $100–$150 |
| Content tools, props, or editing | $50 |
| Community or shelter pilot | $0–$50 |

At a $5 pack price, broad cold advertising is unlikely to work on the first
purchase alone. Calculate the real contribution margin after payment fees, tax
handling, refunds, and AI costs. Set the acquisition ceiling from that number;
do not assume the full $5 is available.

## 9. Experiments backlog

Run one major variable at a time and require a clear success metric.

1. **Hook:** "You know that look" versus "What is your pet thinking?"
2. **Proof:** Finished card first versus photo-to-card reveal.
3. **Value:** "$1 per attempt" versus "Five attempts for $5."
4. **Risk reversal:** No-subscription message versus refund guarantee.
5. **Audience:** Single-pet story versus five-pet household set.
6. **Creator format:** Owner reaction versus narrated card reveal.
7. **CTA:** "Make their card" versus "See their rarity."
8. **Play listing:** Emotional screenshots versus step-by-step screenshots.

For each experiment, record the hypothesis, start date, audience, cost,
conversion result, and decision: stop, revise, or scale.
